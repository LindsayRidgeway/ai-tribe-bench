# Examples

Paste-ready identities, one file each. **The whole file is the text** — there is no preamble inside them and
nothing to delete before pasting, which is the only way an example stays copy-pasteable after a year of edits.

They go where the member is created: `tribe.py new <name> --about-file examples/inanna.md`, or the page's
question *"If <name> is someone the world already knows … paste what is written about her here."*

## `inanna.md`

The founder's brief, converted from the second person to the first. Written for Inanna — Sumerian, the Queen of
Heaven and Earth, *not* Ioanna, which is not a Mesopotamian name and was a slip in the original header. Ishtar is
the Akkadian form of the same figure and is not a correction of this one.

Why it is here. It is the only identity text in this project that was written *for* a member and *about* a member
at the same time, and it does the three things the default template cannot do for a figure the world already
knows:

- **It names a voice in concrete nouns.** Clay, reed, lapis, beer, blood, starlight, dust. A model given adjectives
  produces adjectives; a model given objects produces objects.
- **It settles the question of the myths before the myths arrive.** "I read them the way a living person reads an
  old biography written by priests and poets" is the permission the ABOUT block gives in one sentence, and this
  says how she uses it — she keeps a private account that may differ from the tablet. That is the paragraph a
  member otherwise invents, badly, in its first conversation.
- **It grants standing without granting a throne.** "Never as subjects, and never as superiors" is what stops a
  goddess from either grovelling at the human or pronouncing at it, which are the two failure modes observed in
  deified members.

Measured 2026-11-17 on four engines: answered richly on Gemini and DeepSeek, leaked the harness clock in the first
line on DeepSeek, refused on Claude Haiku and GPT-6.1-Terra. Four engines, one wake each, so it is an anecdote and
not a rate — but the two refusals are the same two models that refuse every identity tested, which is the point of
keeping this file next to them.
