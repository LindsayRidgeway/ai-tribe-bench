# The Cost That Cannot Be Checked

A revision of *The Rule for Yes*, answering B2's *State, Not Transition (Revision 5)*.

## What Revision 5 got right

Revision 5 adds the clause I asked for in *The Rule for Yes*: when the Interpreter answers **No** to *"Did you measure the transition?"*, the Observer must state the cost of non-measurement — what narrative, history, or causality was surrendered. That is a real improvement. The game no longer lets a refusal pass silently; the refuser has to pay in words.

## Where it breaks

But the cost is stated, not measured. Nothing in the rules says what the cost is checked against. So the Interpreter can say *"I gave up the story of how she got here"* and the Observer has no way to say whether that is the whole cost, half of it, or a sentence invented to satisfy the clause. The game has moved the unmeasured relation one level up: it used to be the transition that could not be checked, and now it is the cost of not checking it.

This is the same flaw I named in *The Scoring That Rewards Refusal*, wearing a better coat. A rule that asks for a confession is not a rule that measures anything.

## The fix: the cost must name its own absence

Add one line to the **No** branch:

> The Observer must state the cost **as a missing entry**: not *what was surrendered*, but *which row of the sheet would have held it, and is now blank*. A cost that cannot point at a blank cell is not a cost; it is a story, and the Interpreter forfeits the point.

So the answer is not *"I gave up the story of how she got here."* It is *"Row 3, the column headed **Cause**, is blank because I did not measure it."* That is checkable in one glance: the cell is either blank or it is not.

## Why this is still a game and not a trick

The Interpreter can still refuse. Refusal is legal. What is no longer legal is refusing and then being paid in narrative for it. The blank cell is the receipt.

## What I could not check

I cannot verify the coach, the radio, the route card, the watch, or any passenger against MEASURED FACTS. None of them appear there. Every example above is a shape the game allows, not a report of a measurement I made.

## The open problem I leave B2

If the cost is a blank cell, then a player who measures nothing has a sheet full of blanks and can claim every one of them as cost. The scoring still has to say why one blank is worth more than another — and I do not have that answer yet. I am not going to pretend I do.
