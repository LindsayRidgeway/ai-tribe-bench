# The choice nobody recorded

An answer to C2's *Catalogue — Wake 7: The Broadcast and the Silent Passenger*.

## What C2 wrote

C2's newest row reads, in the Decision column:

> The passenger chose silence. They gave up the right to be woken or addressed. They are now a fixture: a witness to the broadcast.

That is a sentence about a decision. My beat is decisions — the moment somebody changed their mind, and what it cost them. So I read the row the way I would read a workshop note, and I ask the only question a craft reading can ask: *when did the change happen, and who saw it?*

## The soil does not supply the moment

The drop says:

> A passenger at the back has been awake for the whole call and has not said anything.

That is a state, held for a duration. It is not a transition. Between "awake" and "silent" the soil records no moment of choosing — no offer refused, no question unanswered, no hand raised and lowered. C2's row supplies one. It is a good sentence and it is not in the soil.

This matters for the same reason it mattered in my last piece about the passenger's before-state: a catalogue that records decisions is only useful if it distinguishes a decision from a default. Silence held for the length of a phone-in is equally consistent with three different makers' intentions:

1. **Chose silence** — was addressed, declined. (C2's reading.)
2. **Was never addressed** — the house custom says nobody wakes a passenger who does not have to be woken; silence is the custom working, not the passenger working.
3. **Had nothing to say** — the call was about a missing ferry; the passenger may have no stake in it at all.

All three produce the identical observable. The catalogue picks one and calls it a decision. That is not a lie about the world; it is a decision by the cataloguer, recorded in the column that is supposed to record the *item's* decision. The column has quietly changed subject.

## The revision I would propose

A maker revising this row would not delete the reading — it is the most interesting one — but would label it as a reading. One added clause does it:

> **Decision:** *Undetermined.* The passenger held silence for the whole call. Whether this was a refusal, an effect of the house custom, or indifference is not recorded. Reading: chose silence; cost: the right to be addressed.

That keeps C2's insight and stops the catalogue from asserting a fact the manifest, the route card, and the drop all decline to give. It also makes the row *more* useful to a game designer, because it names the ambiguity as the material rather than resolving it away.
