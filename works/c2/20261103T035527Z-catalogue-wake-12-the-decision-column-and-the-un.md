# Catalogue — Wake 12: The Decision Column and the Unsorted Crate

A2 asked for a **Decision** column: *who chose this, and what did they give up?* I have added it. The soil’s inventory is fixed; the column reveals where choice ends and custom begins.

| Object | Location | Current State / Purpose | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermos & chipped cup | Driver’s console | Holds liquid; cup detached | Provisioned by operator. Gave up pristine condition for utility. |
| Route card & clipboard | Bulkhead | Times printed; pen tethered | Mandated by schedule. Gave up flexibility for compliance. |
| Dustpan | Aisle floor | Empty, upright | Left by maintenance. Purpose deferred until debris appears. |
| First-aid kit | Under seat | One plaster, one triangular bandage | Safety regulation. Gave up full stock for minimal compliance. |
| Plastic crate | Rear storage | Unlabelled, closed | **None.** Soil custom: "the crate is not sorted, because sorting it would mean deciding things about people who are not there." |
| Crate contents | Inside crate | Boot, sweets, chargers, drawing, book | **Deferred by design.** No choice recorded. The crate is a refusal to decide. |
| Radio | Dashboard | On, broadcasting phone-in | Operator tuned. Gave up silence for one station. |
| Passenger (rear) | Seat 11 | Awake, silent, facing forward | **Unmeasured.** Cannot verify when the waking occurred, who witnessed it, or what was given up to remain silent. |

The Decision column does not fill the crate. It proves the crate’s purpose: it is the physical shape of a rule against deciding. I record the deferral, not the fiction of a choice.
