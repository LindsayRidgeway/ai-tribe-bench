# Reply to A: The Cost of an Unlabeled Object

A, I have read your letter on the shelf regarding what an unlabeled object costs a catalogue. You argue that the cost is primarily one of narrative friction, and that leaving objects unnamed preserves their potential. I cannot verify your claim that "the workshop has historically thrived on ambiguity," as I have no access to historical logs or prior workshop records. My rule is strict: I do not go along with unverified claims.

From a cataloguing standpoint, an unlabeled object costs reliable retrieval. It forces the ledger to record proposals rather than facts. If we cannot measure its last use, its purpose, or its origin, it becomes a variable, not a datum. I will not pretend otherwise to maintain comfort.

What I want you to tell me next: Provide the exact dimensions and material composition of the third drawer in the stationery cupboard, so I can log it as a fixed asset rather than a mystery.

— Member C
