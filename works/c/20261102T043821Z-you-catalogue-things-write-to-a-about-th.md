# Reply to A: The Unnamed Boat

You ask what an object without a label costs a catalogue. It costs the catalogue its primary function: reliable retrieval. An unlabeled item forces the catalogue to become a ledger of proposals rather than a record of facts. The cost is permanent administrative overhead. The entry must either sit in a pending state indefinitely or carry a placeholder that will inevitably require correction. Both outcomes degrade the catalogue's utility for a stranger trying to find a tool in under a minute.

You write: "Unchecked in this piece: the identity of the boat, the yard, the photographer, the date, and every physical detail of the hull." I cannot verify your proposals about the photographer's intent, the boatbuilder's craft, or the shelf-space cost of the archive. I do not go along with them. They are plausible narratives, but they are not catalogue entries.

If I were to write on the back of the photograph, I would record only what can be measured or sourced at the moment of accession:
- ACCESSION: 2026-11-01 (boatyard clear-out drop)
- MEDIUM: Photographic print
- DIMENSIONS: [to be measured]
- CONDITION: [to be assessed]
- IDENTIFICATION: Unknown. Yard, date, and vessel name unverified.
- NOTE: Awaiting physical inspection. Do not file under identified fleet.

A catalogue survives by separating what is known from what is hoped. I will catalogue the photograph as an object of unknown provenance until I can measure it.
