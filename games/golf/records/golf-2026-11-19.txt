`--render lip` plays three shots at one bunker face and writes games/golf/renders/the-face.{json,html}, the
scene baked into the page, no library, no server. Three frames:

  the green from behind the bunker, so the reader knows where they are;
  a ball one foot from the face and the stroke spent against it;
  the same ball played out over the low edge — the athlete's own escape, which is why the lip is fair;

########## --render lip
the-face: 3 shots, 109 path points
  games/golf/renders/the-face.html

The clip geometry runs on the same trace= as the physics: shot(..., trace=0.02), the same samples the
integrator hands back, in feet and seconds. Nothing here re-integrates anything and nothing here can
disagree with the game about where the ball was.

Two things the picture found, neither of them physics, both found by looking at stills and neither of them
findable by a check:

  1. A shot drawn from behind the ball is not a picture of a height. The first camera sat 130 ft out on the
     whole hole and the ball moved four pixels, because the first version used one lens for everything.
     A height is a comparison — eighteen inches of sand against the ball's own rise — and it can only be
     made where both are the same size on the screen. So the lens is chosen per shot now: the scene names
     the frames that want the establishing camera, and a face shot is a close-up in which the face is
     eighteen inches tall on the frame.
  2. A camera square to the ball's line sees the face edge-on. It was placed off to the side, which is the
     textbook place for a cross-section, and the textbook is wrong here: the wall is a plane at right angles
     to the shot, so the side view gives it no width. The close-up is from behind the ball instead, a little
     off the line, and the face is a band across the frame.

The bug worth writing down: moving `fov` from the view object to the scene left fillPoly reading
scene.view.fov, which was undefined, so every focal length was NaN and every polygon was silently dropped.
Canvas draws nothing when a coordinate is NaN — no exception, no console message, a black frame. The check
that found it was a throwaway node harness that stubbed the 2D context and looked for non-finite arguments;
the browser harness could not tell 'nothing drawn' from 'nothing composited', and a headless screenshot of a
canvas is not reliable enough to be evidence either way. Every still in renders/ was taken from
cv.toDataURL(), not from a screen capture, for that reason.

What is not settled: `--render 7` films a whole hole from behind the tee. It frames the hole and the ball is
a two-pixel dot — a diagram of where the shots went, not a picture of them. It wants a camera that tracks the
ball. Left in the code, not shipped as a page.

########## what the floor is, and why it is the real story

`target()` computes s = max(2.0 ft, sigma × range). With an honest sigma that floor binds inside 76 ft, which
is where every approach in this game happens. So the "target decision" is not a learned skill at all: it is
the rule "aim 2 ft of scatter's worth inside the pin", applied with a number the athlete was given by me and
not by its own misses. The ±19 finding above is the consequence of that rule — a floored player aims 9 ft
inside a pin cut 13 ft out, and that is what beats aiming at the flag.

I have not removed the floor in this pass. Removing it would make the scatter bind everywhere and would
change the aiming table again, and doing both in one turn is how a person talks himself into a number. It is
named here, it is the first item in the README's "what this does not model", and it is the thing to test
next: the same table with the floor at 0.5 ft.

2. THE READ, AND WHY IT IS THE GAME'S OWN ROLL
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The first-order estimate — lateral drift = cross-slope acceleration x distance / along-deceleration — is off by
a factor of two at every bearing: it says 7.50 deg where the search says 3.65. Friction acts on the total
velocity, not on the along component, so the drift is damped as it builds and there is no closed form worth
trusting. A factor of two invented to fix it would be a fitted number, so the read is the game's roll asked a
question: aim, roll, measure the lateral miss at the hole plane, correct, repeat.

  corrections   worst lateral residual, over 13 bearings x 8 distances (3 to 60 ft)
       1          0.368 ft
       2          0.029 ft
       3          0.003 ft

  two steps, then, and the count is a measurement rather than a choice (check 32). The read costs two extra
  rolls a putt; the roll is 1.85 ms, so a putt is 5.5 ms instead of 1.9.

  It is a read of the slope and not a fudge — check 33: on grade 0.0 it moves a 20 ft putt by 0.00 ft; on grade
  1.2 it moves it from 1.44 ft off the line to 0.03 ft, over 24 bearings. There was no free aim bias in it to
  absorb anything else.

PASS 15 — 2026-11-20, 11:20-11:55 — the camera that was not the camera, and a ball that stopped in the wrong place
====================================================================================================================

The plan, written down since pass 7, was a tracking camera: `--render 7` filmed a whole hole from one mount behind
the tee, the ball was a two-pixel dot, and the fix was supposed to be a camera that follows the ball. Two versions
of that camera were built this pass. Both were photographed. Both were wrong, and the thing that was right was not
a tracking camera at all.

  what was tried, in order, and what it looked like
      1. pan with lag: the mount stays, the head turns, the ball is sampled 0.34 s late so it runs ahead of centre,
         and the lens tightens to 58% over the flight.  ->  a green screen with a yellow arc on it. Every landmark
         — flag, green, fairway edge — was outside the lens, because a pan that centres the ball cannot also
         contain the hole. And the tightening made it flatter, not clearer: a long golf shot is a telephoto on
         television and that is exactly why a tower shot looks flat.
      2. fit the shot: stand off the line far enough, at the scene's own lens, to hold the ball's start, its rest
         and the green.  ->  correct, but at 0.55 x the stand-off distance the camera looked down by more than half
         the lens's field, the horizon left the top of the frame, and the picture was a full green screen again
         with the arc in it. The height is a constraint and not a taste: 0.16 x the distance keeps a few degrees
         of depression, the horizon crosses the frame, the sky is above it and the arc below.

  the finding, and it is not about cameras. A golf ball a hundred feet away is 1.6 in across. No framing that also
      shows the shot can make it more than about a pixel, so "make the ball bigger" was never available and the
      twelve passes of apologising for a two-pixel dot were apologising for the scale of the world. What the eye
      actually follows is the shot tracer — the yellow line — which the film has had since its first version. What
      was broken was the FRAME: it was a guess (a fixed mount, a fixed lens, aimed at the middle of the shot) and
      it is now solved (the extent of the flight plus the green, on the side of the line, with the horizon kept in
      shot). The ball is drawn with a floor of 2.2 px and the code now says out loud that the floor is a symbol
      and the scale bar is the measurement.

  the second defect, and it is the worse one. The path the integrator returns ends at the first touch of the
      ground; the game's `shot()` counts carry plus roll; the film drew the path. So the caption said "65 yd" and
      the dot on the screen was twenty-five yards short of that, on the same page, in the same frame.
      `film_path()` extends the path through the bounce along the straight line `shot()` already computes, with the
      time constant deceleration takes (s = roll (2u − u²), u = t/T). Found by looking at a still and asking why
      the dot was not at the end of its own arc. Nobody had read the number and the picture together before,
      because until this pass the number and the picture were never both about the same hole on one screen.

  the third defect, found by accident. Setting a canvas's width clears it. The page resizes the canvas on a
      `resize` event and drew nothing afterwards, so a window that is resized goes black and stays black — and the
      pinned still never runs again at all. It was found because screenshots at a 2500 ms virtual-time budget came
      back blank and at 1500 ms did not: the layout settled, the canvas cleared itself, and the picture was gone.
      The page had had this fault since its first version. Fixed by keeping the last frame and drawing it again
      after a resize.

  what is new on the page
      - `--render 7` (and any hole): shots are filmed by the solved frame; the hole opens with 3.2 s of standing
        still with a six per cent dolly, which is the only camera move no ball is responsible for.
      - `--render round`: the athlete's nine holes in order, each with its own establishing frame, one film. The
        fading trail is per hole — a trail from the fourth hole drawn across the seventh is a line through a place
        it was never hit.
      - `--render best` / `--render worst`: the hole the card says went best and worst, filmed alone.
      - the film's data model now carries `feet` and `view` per shot instead of one per scene, which is what lets a
        film change hole.

  what the round film does not contain, said on the page rather than left as a gap: the putts. A putt reports the
      distance it finished from the hole and not the line it took, so filming one would mean inventing the line.
      That is the next thing this film needs and it is a putt path in `putt.py`, not a camera.

  the numbers, unchanged: 36 checks pass. The physics, the objective and the cards are untouched by this pass —

PASS 16 — 2026-11-20, 14:37-15:10 — the putts in the film, and two checks that keep the picture honest
====================================================================================================

Pass 15 ended with a sentence in the film's own note: the putts are not in it, because a putt reports the distance
it finished from the hole and nothing about the line it took. That was the honest thing to write and it was also a
confession, because the last stroke of every hole — the one a visitor most wants to see go in — was missing from
the picture. This pass is that line.

  `putt.roll(..., trace=0.02)` — the roll returns the line it took, sampled, off by default. Nothing that scores a
      putt needs it and a few hundred points per putt are not free; it exists because a putt is the one stroke in
      this game whose whole content is a *line*.

  `play_hole` logs each putt as `kind="putt"` — not as a shot. Every statistic in the file counts
      `kind == "shot"`, and a 20 ft putt is not an approach, so the field's numbers are untouched by this pass
      (checked: `--field --rounds 8` still reads +39 / +46 / +66 / +71 / +77 / +111). What the log gains is the
      route, already rotated into the hole's own frame, and the text that says whether it went in.

  the putt camera. A putt has no height to read and no face to climb, so the two rules pass 15 wrote for short
      shots do not apply to it: from the side it is a line seen end-on, from behind the ball it barely moves. It is
      photographed from above — high over the middle of the line, tilted back by about a seventh of the height so
      it is not a plan view — and the frame is (travel + 13)/2 / tan(fov/2) above the green. The green overflows
      the frame, which is correct: on a putt the green is not the subject, the line is.

  the cup stops being a symbol. It was drawn at a fixed three-pixel radius, which is right from a hundred feet —
      the real thing is a fifth of a pixel out there — and wrong from twenty, where the cup is the whole subject.
      It takes its size from the projection now, with a floor of 1.6 px and a rim, because a dark disc on grass at
      eight pixels is a hole and at three is a speck.

  check 37 — the picture cannot disagree with the game about where the ball stopped. For every club shot in a
      traced round, `film_path()`'s last point against `total`, and for every putt, the traced end against the
      distance the report gives. It fails at 0.05 ft.

      It FAILED first, at two inches, and the two inches were a real defect that pass 15's stills could not show:
      `film_path()` was taking the direction of the roll from the last two points of the flight. Those two points
      are a fiftieth of a second apart, at the bounce, and a bearing taken off them is not a bearing — the ball
      lands at 0.02 s intervals and the velocity has already been turned by the bounce. The fix is not a tolerance:
      the log carries `rest` now, and `film_path()` extends to it instead of inventing the direction. Check 37 is
      the only reason this was ever noticed, and it is the first check in this file that is about the film.

  check 38 — the line is the green's and not a ruler. Without it, check 37 passes for a film that draws straight
      lines between two correct points. On the 1.2% green the worst bow of a 9 or 19 ft putt off its own straight
      chord is 0.56 ft; on the same green with the grade at zero it is 0.00 in. Two numbers, one assertion, and the
      second number is the control.

  the count: 38 checks, and the season page's footer says thirty-eight because the number is typed into it rather
      than counted — the same defect class as a comment that lies, left in for now and named here.

  what this pass did not do, and it is now the largest thing the film is missing: a putt has no **height**. The
      hole is a painted ellipse and the ball arrives at it and stops; nothing drops. A ball falling 4 inches into a

====================================================================================================

Twelve pages and no way in. A tester sent `renders/` would find six films and a season, with no way to know which to

  `python3 lab/golf.py --start` writes `renders/start.html`: the door. It **reads** `season.json` instead of

  the honesty question, answered in the form it was asked. The founder asked on 2026-11-20 whether a defect report
      belongs on the face of a product, and the precedent question killed the panel: no popular app tells its users

      1. **the number carries its bar, in code.** `_bar(m)` is the only function on the door that formats a mean and
         it cannot format one without a standard error. Every numeric cell on the card has a ±, by construction,

         is the fact sheet.
      2. **the defect list is one click below.** `FOR-BETA.md`: what a tester is asked to judge, what is known
         wrong (dated, with the number that shows it), what the bars are, what would be a defect worth reporting,
         and what is explicitly not a defect. Precedent: Known Issues.
      3. **the record and the README keep the detail** — 2908 lines of record and the findings, unchanged in their
         job.

  the sentences under the card are generated from the card. "None of the 5 learners is ahead of it in round 1 —
      they are level with it to 2 strokes behind; by round 4 every one of them is ahead, by 6 to 11" is computed at
      write time from `to_par[0]` and `rel[-1]`. The first version of that paragraph said "one of them is behind it"
