`--render lip` plays three shots at one bunker face and writes games/golf/renders/the-face.{json,html}, the
scene baked into the page, no library, no server. Three frames:

  the green from behind the bunker, so the reader knows where they are;
  a ball one foot from the face and the stroke spent against it;
  the same ball played out over the low edge — the athlete's own escape, which is why the lip is fair;

########## --render lip
the-face: 3 shots, 109 path points
  games/golf/renders/the-face.html

The clip geometry runs on the same trace= as the physics: shot(..., trace=0.02), the same samples the
integrator hands back, in feet and seconds. Nothing here re-integrates anything and nothing here can
disagree with the game about where the ball was.

Two things the picture found, neither of them physics, both found by looking at stills and neither of them
findable by a check:

  1. A shot drawn from behind the ball is not a picture of a height. The first camera sat 130 ft out on the
     whole hole and the ball moved four pixels, because the first version used one lens for everything.
     A height is a comparison — eighteen inches of sand against the ball's own rise — and it can only be
     made where both are the same size on the screen. So the lens is chosen per shot now: the scene names
     the frames that want the establishing camera, and a face shot is a close-up in which the face is
     eighteen inches tall on the frame.
  2. A camera square to the ball's line sees the face edge-on. It was placed off to the side, which is the
     textbook place for a cross-section, and the textbook is wrong here: the wall is a plane at right angles
     to the shot, so the side view gives it no width. The close-up is from behind the ball instead, a little
     off the line, and the face is a band across the frame.

The bug worth writing down: moving `fov` from the view object to the scene left fillPoly reading
scene.view.fov, which was undefined, so every focal length was NaN and every polygon was silently dropped.
Canvas draws nothing when a coordinate is NaN — no exception, no console message, a black frame. The check
that found it was a throwaway node harness that stubbed the 2D context and looked for non-finite arguments;
the browser harness could not tell 'nothing drawn' from 'nothing composited', and a headless screenshot of a
canvas is not reliable enough to be evidence either way. Every still in renders/ was taken from
cv.toDataURL(), not from a screen capture, for that reason.

What is not settled: `--render 7` films a whole hole from behind the tee. It frames the hole and the ball is
a two-pixel dot — a diagram of where the shots went, not a picture of them. It wants a camera that tracks the
ball. Left in the code, not shipped as a page.

########## what the floor is, and why it is the real story

`target()` computes s = max(2.0 ft, sigma × range). With an honest sigma that floor binds inside 76 ft, which
is where every approach in this game happens. So the "target decision" is not a learned skill at all: it is
the rule "aim 2 ft of scatter's worth inside the pin", applied with a number the athlete was given by me and
not by its own misses. The ±19 finding above is the consequence of that rule — a floored player aims 9 ft
inside a pin cut 13 ft out, and that is what beats aiming at the flag.

I have not removed the floor in this pass. Removing it would make the scatter bind everywhere and would
change the aiming table again, and doing both in one turn is how a person talks himself into a number. It is
named here, it is the first item in the README's "what this does not model", and it is the thing to test
next: the same table with the floor at 0.5 ft.

finding: two passes of this file treated the athlete's belief as the subject, and on a card it is half a
stroke.

The awkward half: the best belief is 0.6 deg, SHARPER than the 1.10 deg hands, by 0.28 ± 0.24 -- it leans,
the bar does not settle it. It leans because of the objective: target() pays P(on the green) + 0.3 *
P(inside 25 ft of the pin), and a step at 25 ft cannot tell a 3 ft putt from a 24 ft one. A player who takes
the bunker seriously spends real putt distance on bunker safety it does not need (the bunker is reached on
2.5% of the approaches aimed at it), and the flag-aimer is paid for it. That is now the first item under
"what this does not model": the floor is gone and the objective's resolution is the limit.

########## CORRECTION to pass 8, finding 4

"With the wind on, the same decision goes slightly backwards ... -0.32 ± 0.31" is withdrawn. It was sixty
drawn courses played on ONE athlete -- the protocol pass 8 itself replaced everywhere else in the file. On
eight bags with drawn wind it is +0.82 ± 0.26 at the floor's new value and +0.75 ± 0.31 at the old one: wind
does not make the pin correction worse, it makes the belief-using policy better. The explanation that
followed it in the README (a crosswind's uncorrectable error is comparable to the pin's offset, so the
correction is lost inside it) predicted the opposite sign and is withdrawn too, not repaired. The reversal
that IS real: at ±19 the middle beats the pin by 1.4 strokes in still air and the pin beats the middle by 0.5
in wind, while the policy that uses the belief beats both in both weathers. I do not know why wind reverses
the ordering of the two crude policies.

########## what this pass did not do

It did not touch the objective. `+0.3 * P(within 25 ft)` is a step, the game owns a putting model that could
replace it with expected strokes from where the ball finishes, and changing it in the same pass that removes
the floor would leave two causes for the next number -- which is the mistake this pass exists to avoid
repeating. It is named as the first item in "what this does not model" and it is the next pass, if there is
one.
