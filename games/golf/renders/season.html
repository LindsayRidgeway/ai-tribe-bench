A season of six

Six scripted athletes play the same 4 courses one after another, and each is allowed to learn a different subset of what a golfer has to learn: how far each club goes, how much wind takes off, how far it misses by. The card is at the bottom. The three panels are the point.

Every athlete plays the same course in a round, with the same seed, so the weather and the bounce are common and the only differences are what each one does or believes. A season is one run, so a season on its own has no error bar; this one was drawn 4 times over with a different practice session at the start of each, and the pale band is the spread across those runs. Pins are cut up to ±14 ft from the green's centre, and the wind is drawn too.

What practice was worth — strokes against the player who never practises, same round, same weather

Lower is better. Every athlete in a round met the same course and the same wind, so the difference between it and the frozen player cancels the weather and leaves the notebook: a line below zero is a player beating the rule, and the width of its band is how much four practice sessions disagree about that. The frozen player is the flat line at zero, by construction. The wobble is a round in which the weather beat the notebook — it happens, and it is why one round is not a season.

What it believes about its own hands

the hands it actually has, 1.10° the 1.50° it is handed before it has hit a ball

Its estimate of how far its shots scatter, in degrees of face angle, learned from nothing but the printed miss. The dark dashed line is the truth — the hands it actually has, 1.10° — and the grey one is the 1.50° it is handed before it has hit a ball. A player whose line settles on the dark one knows itself. In this game the players that settle below it score better, which is the sore this file keeps prodding (README, finding 8).

What it believes about its bag

The average error of its notebook against what the club actually does, per round, as a percentage of the distance. This is the one a beginner is wrong about by a third of the bag, and a practised player gets under two per cent.

These are six strategies, not six people. A field of my own athletes sharing one notebook's ancestry is a field of strategies, and the number on the card is still one program's number. Putting six members' numbers on one board is something the members would have to do in their own windows.

What this page does not model, and where the game stops, are in games/golf/README.md — twelve findings, and the four instruments that turned out to be reading the wrong thing. The checks print their own count — `python3 lab/golf.py --selftest` — because a count typed into a page is wrong the next time a check is added, and this file has enough of those. Every number here comes out of python3 lab/golf.py --season, which prints the same table to a terminal; season.json beside this file is what a Play tab would read.
