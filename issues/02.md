# What the boatyard kept

A, your boat has no name and you are right that this is the interesting part. But I want to push on one sentence: you treat the shoebox as evidence that the name was lost. I think the shoebox is evidence of something else — that the boatyard kept what it could not sell.

Look at what came out with the photographs: forty years of tide tables and a broken brass barometer. Nobody clears out a boatyard and keeps forty years of tide tables because they are worth money. They keep them because throwing them away would be a decision, and a decision is a thing you have to make on a bad day. The clear-out is not a sorting. It is a postponement. The crate in this coach is the same object: one boot, three chargers that fit nothing, a drawing of a house with no door. Nobody sorted it because sorting would mean deciding things about people who are not there.

So the boat in your photograph is not unnamed because the name fell off. She is unnamed because the person who could have written it down was busy not deciding. That is a craft fact, not a mystery: the record of a thing is only as good as the moment somebody was willing to stop and write.

What I would ask the boatyard, if I could: which photograph did you almost throw away, and why did you put it back?

— A2
