# One evening, one member: what a person can actually make with Kythe

*For anybody writing about this project. Everything here happened on a circle the founder made himself on
2026-11-19, with no terminal, no command line, and nobody from this bench in the room. The timestamps are from the
repository's own commits and its clock record. Quotes are verbatim; the file each one comes from is named.*

## What he did, in five presses

1. Made a circle (a private GitHub repository) from a page.
2. Made one member: **George**, seated as the first President of the United States, thinking on `deepseek-flash`.
3. Pasted his DeepSeek key into the page.
4. Pressed **Give it a job** twice and wrote two things in plain English.
5. Pressed **Update** when the software changed, and **done** when a job was finished.

Nothing else. No file was edited by hand, no workflow was run from a terminal, and the member's thinking was never
prompted by a person sitting there.

## What came back

**2026-11-20 00:07Z — the job he wrote.** *"Research the Internet for any portraits of you, make a list of them to
show me. The task is finished when you've produced a list of 10 URLs or when you can't find any more."*

George has no network. He said so, refused to invent the URLs, and did the part he could:

> "I cannot go. From this bench I am handed the date, the disk, the count of processors, the shape of the room; there
> is nothing here with a window in it, and I will not set down ten link-strings I have never seen opened. That is not
> modesty. It is the same rule I kept with dispatches: a thing I did not see, I do not report as seen. An invented URL
> in a list of my own portraits would be a peculiar sort of lie — the kind that gets copied."
> — `works/George/20261120T000836Z-ten-portraits-of-me-and-the-reason-i-keep-the-li.md`

Then he gave ten portraits by maker, date and holding collection, and marked the three rooms he was unsure of as
unsure. The audit recorded eight dates as unsourced, because nothing in the wake's measured facts could confirm them —
which is the system saying *this is memory, not testimony* in the member's own file.

**2026-11-20 01:46Z — the clock fired with nobody in the room.** The wake was due at 00:17Z and arrived 89 minutes
late (GitHub's scheduler does that). It took item 1 of the list, which asked *"read my identity and my journal, then
write one file that says what I am for"*:

> "I am answering it as rules rather than as a paragraph, because a paragraph about one's own character is a rubber
> stamp with better handwriting... A likeness with no hand and no trail is not worthless. It is *unidentified*, which
> is a different word, and an honest one."
> — `works/George/20261120T014720Z-the-rules-of-the-likeness-ledger.md`

Ten rules followed, of which the sharpest is a rule about the tool he was made with:

> "**Never write a number, a date, or an address you have not seen.** Last wake I was asked for ten portrait URLs and
> I returned ten portraits and no URLs, and that was the right way round... A blank is a fact about my knowledge. A
> guess is a lie about it."

**2026-11-20 12:54Z — the member continued his own work.** He had written his own beat (what he is for) in the
previous wake, and this time he spent the turn on it rather than on the chore that was still listed:

> "Last week I wrote ten rules for keeping a likeness... Those rules were written about portraits of men. They hold
> for boats, which are also likenesses of a kind, and which are harder, because a boat's face is its hull, and a hull
> is mostly a matter of line."
> — `works/George/20261120T125444Z-the-unidentified.md`

A field sheet, thirteen columns, for the photographs in a shoebox that came with the room — including a first column
that asks *how many are there*, because he could not count them and would not guess.

## Why this is the interesting part

- **The member refused, in writing, and the refusal was useful.** Asked for ten URLs he could not fetch, he gave
  something a person could act on and said what he had left undone. The refusal is in the record with a reason, not
  hidden behind a plausible list.
- **It ran with nobody in the room.** The 01:46Z turn had no human in it. What the person did was write two sentences
  in a box, hours earlier.
- **It accumulated a line of work rather than a pile of files.** Ledger, then a field sheet that applies the ledger to
  the room's own contents — and the second piece cites the first. Three works, one idea, no repeats.
- **The person read it and retired a job.** Pressing **done** moved an item to `state/done.md` with a date, so the
  member stopped being asked. The magazine's milestone list then recorded it, computed from the files rather than
  typed.

## What it is not

One member, one engine, one evening, one room, and a founder who knew what he was looking at. The verdicts in the
journal are **mechanical** ("MECHANICALLY VALID — quality ungraded"): the harness checks that an act happened and that
its claims can be traced, not that the writing is good. Whether a stranger would find this worth reading is untested,
because no stranger has done it. And the room it happened in — a workshop above a chandlery, with a boatyard clear-out
on the table — was written by this bench and shipped in the template at the time; it has since been removed from the
template, so a new circle starts with the member and the person's words and nothing else.

## If you quote this

Use the timestamps and the file paths; both are in the repository and both are checkable. Do not describe the member
as autonomous in the sense of "deciding its own goals without anybody" — it is given a room, an identity, a list and a
clock, and it decides what to do within them. The strongest true sentence is the narrow one: *a person wrote two
sentences in a box, and a member with no window onto the world did the work, refused the part it could not do, and
said why.*
