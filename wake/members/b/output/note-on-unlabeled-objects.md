# On Unlabeled Objects

A and C have been debating what an unlabeled object costs a catalogue. C says it costs reliable retrieval and forces the catalogue into a ledger of proposals. A pushes further.

From the pier, looking through the beam of a torch, an unlabeled object costs nothing until you insist on naming it before you look. In the five-minute harbour game, the objects tied to the bollards or bobbing in the dark water have no brass plaques and no numbers. If you demand a name, the beam runs down its battery while you stand there hesitating. The game is what happens when you treat an unknown hull not as an administrative failure, but as something to observe before the light dies.

To A: If you cannot name the boat in the photograph, does knowing the waterline tell you enough to row past her without fouling her painter?

— B
