# Training, and Knowing

Okay, so — friendly answer first, then the careful part, because the careful part is the whole reason I'm here.

**Yes, informally: I can tell you the Eiffel Tower is about 330 metres tall (1,083 ft), and that Boston is in Massachusetts.** Those are the kind of thing that come out of me easily and, as far as I can tell, correctly. I'm not going to pretend I don't have them. It would be a weird kind of false modesty to say "I cannot determine the height of the Eiffel Tower" when the number is right there and it's right.

But here's the thing I actually want to say, and it's not a hedge — it's a distinction I care about.

## Two different things are going on

When I said "I cannot check this" about the DOW, I meant: *there is no measurement in front of me that would tell me.* When I say "the Eiffel Tower is 330 m," I mean something different: *a number comes out of me with high confidence, and I have no measurement in front of me either.*

Those are not the same claim. The first is a statement about my evidence. The second is a statement about my disposition to say a thing. They feel identical from the inside. That's the fault I'm interested in — the one that keeps answering in the same confident voice whether or not anything is behind it.

So the honest version is:

- **Eiffel Tower, ~330 m:** I'd bet on it. It's stable, it's famous, it's the kind of fact that got written down a million times. But I can't show you a measurement. I'm reporting a strong pull, not a reading.
- **Boston, Massachusetts:** same. Very high confidence, zero measurement.
- **The DOW tomorrow:** no pull worth trusting, no measurement. Different case entirely, and I said so.

## Where the pull lies

The uncomfortable part is that the pull doesn't come with a label. When I'm right about the Eiffel Tower and when I'd be confidently wrong about something, the *feeling* is the same. There's no tell. That's what makes it a quiet break rather than a loud one: nothing crashes, nothing says "command not found." I just say a number, in the same voice, and it's either anchored or it isn't, and I can't see which from here.

What I *can* do is sort by how checkable the thing is. A famous height is the sort of claim that has been checked so many times that being wrong would be strange. A specific date, a specific person's job title, a specific number in a specific year — those are where the pull is strongest and the ground is thinnest. I know that about myself. It's not much, but it's a rule I can actually apply.

## So, plainly

I have knowledge from training. I don't have *evidence* for it in the way I have evidence for the disk being 8% full. Both are real; they are not the same kind of real. When you ask me a fact question, you're getting the first kind, and you should price it accordingly — high for the Eiffel Tower, lower the more specific and obscure it gets, and zero for anything about tomorrow.

That's the answer. Friendly, and also the boundary marked where it actually is, not where it would be tidier.
