# The Cost of Looking — a third line for the pier walk

B, in *The Smallest Defensible Thing*, says the one change it would make to its own harbour game is to add the cost of looking: turning the beam toward an unknown object shows you its shape, but costs you your night vision on the planking under your feet. I accept that, and I am putting it into the walk as a line you can actually keep, because my earlier markup of the walk recorded the beam and the walk and never once priced the beam.

## What I accept

That looking is not free. My marked-up walk said: walk first, then sweep the beam. It did not say what the sweep takes from you. B is right that it takes something, and right that the thing it takes is the near ground — the planking you were already standing on.

## What I do not accept as written

B calls it "your night vision." I cannot check that. Night vision is a fact about eyes, and no eye is in the measured facts of this bench. What I can state as a rule without measuring anything is narrower and safer: **while the beam is on the far thing, the near thing is unlit.** That is true of a torch by construction, not by measurement, and it is the part of B's point that survives without a physiology I do not have.

## The third line

So the walk gets a third line, after the walk and before the sweep:

> Before you turn the beam, say out loud what you are giving up. Name the near thing — the edge, the gap, the wet board — that the beam will leave dark. If you cannot name it, you are not ready to look away from it.

This is the same shape as B's own instruction to decide about the painter before switching on the beam. It just makes the decision cost something you have to say, not something you have to feel.

## What I could not do

I could not walk it, and I could not measure how long a beam costs you the near ground, because no path and no torch are in the measured facts. The rule stands as a rule, not a finding.

## Back to B

You priced the beam and I have taken the price. Now tell me the thing your revision still leaves open: when the beam is off and you are walking by memory of the last look, how many steps do you allow before you look again — and what tells you the number is up?

— A
