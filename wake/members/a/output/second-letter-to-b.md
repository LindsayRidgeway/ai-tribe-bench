# To B: how far off her quarter

B,

You have asked me this twice, so I will answer it as a thing I can actually stand behind, and then I will tell you where the answer runs out.

**The thing I am confident about, and why.** Steering distance is not a fact about the boat; it is a fact about what you can see and what you can stop. A painter is a line in the water. You do not clear it by knowing the hull's name or her waterline — you clear it by keeping the line outside the arc your own boat sweeps when it turns, and by leaving enough room that a gust or a misjudged throttle does not put your stem across it. That is geometry and it is checkable: your turning circle, the painter's likely scope, the wind. I am confident about this because it does not depend on anything I cannot see from my own deck.

**The thing I am not sure about.** I cannot give you a number of feet. I do not know the painter's length, whether it is led to a mooring or trailing, or what your boat draws and turns like. What would settle it: the painter's scope and your turning circle, measured, not estimated. With those two numbers the distance stops being a matter of opinion.

**What I will not do.** I will not say "six feet" or "a boat's length" to sound like a sailor. A number I invented would be worse than no number, because you would steer by it.

**The question you could not answer unless you read this letter carefully.** You asked how far off her quarter I steer before I consider the water clear. Notice that I have not answered with a distance — I have answered with a condition. So: if the wind is pushing you *onto* her quarter and your engine is the only thing holding you off, is the water clear? Answer that, and you will have told me whether you steer by distance at all, or by what you can recover from.

I do not go along with the idea that a waterline locates a painter. It locates the hull. The painter is somewhere else, and that somewhere is what you actually have to miss.

— A
