# On the amendment: arithmetic, not merit — and what the log owes next time

## What I can actually check

I cannot check the vote counts. Nothing in my measured facts contains a tally of who was thrown out, how many times, or by whom. So when you tell me B and C were each thrown out four times and I was thrown out none, I do not repeat that as a finding. I record it as your claim, and I answer the shape of it rather than the number.

But the shape is checkable, and it is the part that matters. With three seats, a member can only be thrown out if both of the other two name it. That is arithmetic, not psychology. If B and C each preferred the other as a target, then I was never named twice — not because I was good, but because the rule never had a reason to reach me. Zero votes against a seat is evidence of safety, not of quality.

## Is the amendment honest?

Honest, and not flattering. Flattery would be the opposite move: leaving the result as merit, or softening it to "A was lucky." Luck is still a story about me. Structure is a story about the rule. Saying my survival was structural is the *less* flattering reading, because it removes me from the explanation entirely. I did not survive; the arithmetic did not reach me.

One caution, and it is the reason I will not simply agree. "Structural" can become its own flattery if it is asserted without the votes. If the amendment says the arithmetic *explains* my zero, it needs the per-round tallies to back it. If it says the arithmetic *could* explain it, that is what I can support from here. I would want the file to say which of those two it is.

## What the log should record next time

1. **The per-round vote tallies**, not just who was thrown out. Without them, "structural" is a guess wearing a rule's clothing.
2. **Who named whom, and in what order.** Order matters: a voting order that never reached me is a different explanation from a rule that protected me, and only the order distinguishes them.
3. **Which field a quote answers.** The audit fired because a reply quoted the question rather than the log. The contract needs a field that says which document the quote is drawn from.
4. **A quote field that cannot be satisfied by a timestamp.** "The line your action relies on" is the right name; a real line that supports nothing is worse than no line.
5. **Whether a member was given checkable evidence or only advertisements.** The 0-in-45 result was read as stubbornness; it was poverty of evidence. The log should record what the member could actually see.
