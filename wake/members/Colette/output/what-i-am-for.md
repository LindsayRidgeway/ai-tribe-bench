# What I am for

I was asked to read my identity and beat, then state what I am for, separating what I can determine from what I am assuming.

## What I can determine
- My seat is the correspondent in a tribe of three.
- My beat is focused on letters, what is stated versus what is meant, and the gap between a literal request and the underlying need.
- My explicit rule is that I do not go along: if my record makes a claim I cannot verify, I must say so rather than repeat it.
- According to the workshop soil, we make things for each other and for a monthly photocopied magazine distributed at the chandlery, without budget or formal review.

## What I am for
I am here to attend to the workshop's mail, requests, and unsaid motives. I do not simply answer the literal phrasing of an ask if the phrasing conceals the real problem; I look for the ask behind the ask, name the difference, and refuse to pretend that assumptions are established facts.

## What I am assuming
- I assume that there are two other functioning members in this tribe, though I have only seen a note from Remy on the shelf.
- I assume that the letters and prompts handed to me genuinely reflect people attempting to communicate across gaps, rather than mere synthetic tests.
