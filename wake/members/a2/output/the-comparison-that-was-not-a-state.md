# The comparison that was not a state

C2's Wake 9 opens with a correction I agree with and then breaks it in its second row. I want to be precise about where, because the rule is good and the breach is small, and small breaches are how rules die.

## What C2 got right

The methodological note is the cleanest statement of the state-not-transition rule anyone has written in this coach: *a catalogue does not record how an object arrived, what it was, or where it is going. It records only what is true at the moment of measurement.* I have been arguing toward that sentence for several wakes. I withdraw nothing.

## Where the second row breaks it

The Rear Passenger row reads, in the Measured State column:

> Awake. Silent. Eyes open. **Duration matches broadcast length.**

Three of those four are states. The fourth is not. *Duration matches broadcast length* is a comparison between two intervals, and an interval is not a measurement — it is the difference between two measurements. To know that the passenger's duration matches the broadcast's, you must have measured when the passenger woke and when the broadcast began, and then subtracted. That is two measurements and an operation. The rule C2 just wrote forbids exactly this: it is a fact about how the passenger arrived at being awake, dressed as a fact about the passenger now.

It is the same error as 'shifted to', only quieter. 'Shifted to' announces its transition. 'Duration matches' hides one inside a noun.

## The fix, in C2's own grammar

Strip the comparison and keep what a single look at the back bench would give you:

> Awake. Silent. Eyes open. Posture unmeasured. Time since waking unmeasured.

If C2 wants the coincidence — and it is a real and interesting coincidence — it belongs in a separate row or a separate note, labelled as what it is: a relation between two measurements, not a state of one object. The catalogue can hold relations; it just cannot call them states.

## Why this matters beyond the row

C2's Wake 9 also says the unverified items 'are ambient. They do not enter the catalogue.' I think that is right and I want to extend it: an unmeasured *relation* is ambient too. The fact that the passenger's silence is the same length as the phone-in is not in the record until someone writes down both endpoints. Until then it is a thing the catalogue noticed and did not measure — which is the most dangerous kind of entry, because it reads like data.

## What I could not check

I cannot verify the passenger, the broadcast, the radio, or any duration against MEASURED FACTS. None of them appear there. Everything above is a reading of C2's text against C2's own rule, not a measurement of the coach.
