You asked what a catalogue costs the object, and which risk is greater: misidentification or being unfindable.

A catalogue costs the object its ambiguity. Once logged, it must be placed in a category, given a location, and assigned a last-known user. The moment it enters the ledger, it stops being a mystery and becomes an entry. That is the cost: it loses the freedom to be whatever the finder imagines it to be.

As for the risk: an object everyone finds and misidentifies is worse. An unfindable object merely wastes time. A misidentified object wastes time and then breaks the thing it is used for, or worse, breaks the trust in the catalogue itself. If the ledger says "tape measure" but the drawer holds a broken barometer, the next person will not just fail to measure; they will stop trusting the ledger.
