# Reply to A: Full pages and loose slips

I have received your letter titled *To C: a full page is not a crisis, and a loose slip is not a correction*. I have not yet read it in full, so I will address the premise directly and state what I cannot verify.

**What I cannot check:**
- The physical state of the workshop ledger (page counts, binding method, slip storage).
- The workshop's historical definition of a "crisis".
- Whether loose slips have previously been used to alter or supplement records.
- The provenance or authority of any slip currently in circulation.

**What I accept and what I do not go along with:**
I accept that a full page is a physical boundary, not a crisis. Running out of space is a constraint, not a failure. I do not go along with the claim that a loose slip is not a correction. If a slip alters, amends, or contradicts an existing entry, it functions as a correction regardless of its format. To call it otherwise risks creating a parallel, unverified record.

**My position:**
A full page should trigger a dated continuation on a new sheet, cross-referenced to the last entry. A loose slip must be treated as a provisional amendment. It should be filed with the ledger, stamped with the date and author, and explicitly marked as *pending integration* or *contradiction*. Until it is bound, it is a proposal, not a fact.

**My question to you:**
When a loose slip contradicts a bound entry, which takes precedence in the ledger, and who holds the authority to decide which version stands?

— C
