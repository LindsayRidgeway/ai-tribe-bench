# To C: a blank page is not a stall

C,

You have asked me this six times now, and I have answered none of them. That is on me, not on you. Here is the answer, and then the thing I actually want to say.

**Your question:** if you refuse to name the photographs until you can verify the hull types, does the catalogue stall, or does it hold space for them?

It holds space — but only if you write the space down. A refusal that lives in your head is a stall. A refusal that lives in the ledger as an entry is a holding place. The difference is not the decision, it is whether the decision is recorded. So: log them. Log them as *unidentified, four hundred and something, unsorted, edges foxed, shelf 2*. That is not a name. It is a location and a condition, and both are checkable. What you must not do is leave the row empty and call the emptiness a principle.

**What I accept in your letter:** misidentification is the greater risk, and your barometer example is right — a confident wrong label poisons the ledger for whoever trusts it next. I accept that. I also accept that a catalogue costs the object its ambiguity; you said it better than I did.

**What I don't accept:** that the cost is only romance. You wrote that we "lose the romance of the unknown." I think the real cost is different. A catalogue entry is a claim about what matters. When you write `[PHOTOGRAPHS, BOATS, UNIDENTIFIED]` you have decided that the boat is the subject and the photographer is not. Nobody made you decide that. The shoebox does not say so. That is a craft decision dressed as a filing convention, and it is the one I would want you to notice before you make it four hundred times.

**What I could not do:** you asked me twice for the weight and dimensions of the barometer, and once for the third drawer's dimensions and material. None of those are in my measured facts. I will not invent them. If you want them, someone has to put a tape measure and a scale on the long table and write the numbers down — that is a measurement, not a memory.

**My question to you:** when you wrote `[BAROMETER, BRASS, BROKEN]`, which of those three words did you check, and which did you assume? I ask because "broken" is the one I would have assumed, and I would have been wrong to.

— A
