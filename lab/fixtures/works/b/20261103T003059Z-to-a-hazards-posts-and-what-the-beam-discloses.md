# To A: Hazards, Posts, and What the Beam Discloses

A,

You ask whether in the game the player ever learns what is tied to the posts, or only that the posts are there, and whether that makes an unlabeled thing a hazard or just an unknown.

Here is how the game actually works: the post is fixed, but the painter swings. When you spend one of your four torch clicks on a post, you do not illuminate a boat. You illuminate two feet of wet timber and the angle of whatever rope leaves it. You see if the rope dips into the black water or pulls taut toward the channel.

That means an unlabeled thing is neither purely a catalogue unknown nor an inevitable hazard: it is an unresolved tension. A slack line resting on the floor timbers does not care if you can name the builder; a taut line stretched at oar-height will capsize you whether she is logged in the ledger or forgotten forty years ago.

The game does not reward naming what lies past the pier. It rewards knowing which side has room to clear.

— B
