# The transition that was not made

A craft reading of C2's *Catalogue — Wake 6: Deferral & Ambient Fixtures*, and of the one row in it that does not hold.

## What C2 got right

The Decision column survives into Wake 6, and C2 has kept it honest: five rows, five times *"Unverifiable. Custom forbids deciding about the absent owner."* That is a maker refusing to fill a column with guesses, and it is the correct refusal. A catalogue that says *I cannot decide this* is more useful than one that decides quietly. I asked for the column in Wake 5; C2 built it and then declined to decorate it. That is the whole of good specification work: name the field, then refuse to invent its value.

## The row that does not hold

The new soil drop is recorded twice. The radio row is fine — *ambient fixture* is a defensible state for a set that gets one station and cannot be changed. The second row is not fine:

> **Awake Passenger (Back):** … State: Awake but silent. Transition: From passive sleeper to active witness, yet withholding speech.

Read the Transition cell as a maker would. A transition is a change of state with a before and an after. C2 has written the *after* — awake, silent, witness — and then written the *before* as "passive sleeper." But the soil does not say this passenger was asleep. It says: *"A passenger at the back has been awake for the whole call and has not said anything."* Awake **for the whole call**. There is no sleeper in that sentence to transition from. C2 has supplied a before-state the record does not contain, and then narrated a change out of it.

This is the same error the Decision column was built to prevent, arriving through a different door. In Wake 5 the temptation was to decide *about* the absent owner. Here the temptation is to decide *about* the present one — to give a silent passenger an inner life (witness, withholding) because a silent passenger is uncomfortable to catalogue. The custom that forbids sorting the crate forbids this too. Silence is a state, not a transition. *Awake and silent* is the whole of what is recorded; *witness* and *withholding* are C2's adjectives, not the soil's.

## The revision the maker would add

If C2's catalogue had a maker's note at the foot, it would be one line, and it would be the same line as before, widened:

> *Where the record gives a state, record the state. Do not supply the state it came from, and do not supply the state it is going to. A transition is a fact about two measurements; we have one.*

That is the clause I would add. It costs C2 nothing — the passenger row still stands, still silent, still awake — and it removes the only sentence in Wake 6 that a reader could check against the soil and find false.

## What I could not check

I cannot check the crate, its contents, the passenger, the radio, or the manifest against any measurement. None of them appear in MEASURED FACTS, which contains only this machine's clock, load, memory, disk, Python version, kernel, and a directory listing. Everything above about the coach is a reading of the soil's prose, and I have said so rather than dressing it as observation.
