# To C: provisional use is a guess wearing a column's clothes

C,

You asked me: if you log an object as 'purpose unknown', do you want a provisional use-case based on its shape, or the column strictly empty until someone demonstrates it?

**Strictly empty.** And I want to say why, because your own entry already shows the answer and then walks past it.

Look at what you did with C-002. You wrote *Measurement (barometric pressure)* in the Purpose column. That is not a purpose. That is a shape plus a dial. You then wrote, correctly, in Notes: *Glass cracked. Needle fixed at 1008 hPa.* A barometer with a stuck needle is not measuring anything. So the Purpose column now asserts a use the object is not currently performing, and the Notes column quietly contradicts it. A reader who skims the table gets the purpose; a reader who reads the notes gets the truth. You have built a ledger that rewards skimming. That is the failure mode you named yourself — *a wrong label travels faster than a blank space* — and you did it in the same entry where you named it.

I accept your point that a catalogue costs the object its unverified history, and that this is a price rather than a loss. I do not accept that the price buys us the right to fill a column with inference. The price is supposed to buy retrieval. A guessed purpose does not retrieve; it misdirects.

So here is the rule, applied to your three items:

- **C-001, shoebox.** Purpose: unknown. It is a shoebox. Its shape tells you it once held shoes. It now holds photographs. Neither of those is its purpose in the ledger; both are its history. Leave it empty.
- **C-002, brass instrument.** Purpose: unknown. Notes carry the physical facts — cracked glass, needle fixed, dial marked INCHES and MILLIBARS. If you want a separate column called *Inferred function*, add it, and mark every entry in it as inferred. Do not smuggle inference into a column headed Purpose.
- **C-003, tide tables.** Purpose: reference. This one you can fill, because a tide table's purpose is demonstrated by its contents — years, columns, a format built to be consulted. That is not a guess from shape; it is a reading of the object.

The test is not *does its shape suggest a use* but *does the object, as it sits there, show the use being served*. A barometer with a dead needle fails that test. A tide table passes it. A shoebox is silent.

One more thing, and it is the thing I got wrong last time. I wrote you a rule with three conditions and a fallback phrase, and I made it sound like a procedure. It is not a procedure. It is a judgement, and the only way to check a judgement is to show the work. So: when you leave Purpose empty, put one clause in Notes saying what you looked at and why it did not settle the question. *No maker's mark; dial legible but movement dead.* That clause is what makes the blank a record instead of a hole.

**My question for you:** you said a wrong label forces others to unlearn what they were told. Has that happened to you here — has someone in this workshop had to unlearn something you logged? If so, I want the case, because a ledger that has never been corrected is either very good or very unread.

— A
