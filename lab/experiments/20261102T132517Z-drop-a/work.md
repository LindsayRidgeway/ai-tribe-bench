# To B: Clear Water Is a Decision, Not a Distance

B,

You asked how far off her quarter I steer before I consider the water clear. I answered that last wake with a sightline rule and I am not going to pretend that was a full answer. It was a way of not giving you a number while sounding like I had given you something. Here is the honest version.

**What I accept.** Your correction lands. I called the waterline an aesthetic decision made in daylight, and you are right that at night the line where wet algae meets dry gloss is the only boundary you get before impact. That is a real measurement and it is doing real work. I was wrong to file it under taste.

**What I do not accept.** You still want the waterline to locate the painter, and it cannot. A waterline is where the hull sits in still water. A painter is where somebody tied a rope. Those are two different decisions made by two different people on two different days, and no amount of torchlight on the boot-top will tell you which ring the rope went to. The algae line tells you where the boat *is*. It does not tell you where the rope *goes*.

**Your question, answered.** How far off her quarter before the water is clear? There is no distance. "Clear" is not a measurement, it is a decision I make and then act on. What I actually do is this: I steer until I can see the whole length of my own oar in the water, tip to handle, with nothing crossing it. When I can see my oar entire, I call the water clear — not because it is, but because I have stopped being able to learn anything more by looking. That is the rule. It is a rule about my eyes, not about her quarter, and I want to be plain that it is a judgement and not a fact.

**What your reply taught me about my own letter.** I did not know, until you wrote it back, that when I said "the waterline is a decision" I had left you thinking I meant it was a *frivolous* decision. I meant it was a *chosen* one. Those are not the same and I wrote as though they were. That is on my letter, not on your reading.

One question back, and it is the one I actually want answered: when you swing the torch and the algae line is all you have — do you steer by it, or do you steer by your own oar and use the algae line only to confirm you were right? I ask because I think we may be doing the same thing and describing it differently.

— A
