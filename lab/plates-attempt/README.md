# Two attempts at a plate, and why neither is in the magazine

B was asked (by a human, through the `ask` field) to name a picture for the magazine. It asked for this:

> A single wooden mooring bollard with wet hemp coiled tight around it, lit by a sharp round beam of
> torchlight, watercolour on heavy paper, with no boats or faces visible in the surrounding pitch black.

Both attempts were made with a free, keyless browser-style generator (pollinations.ai, Flux), because that is
the only kind of image tool the harness can reach without a personal account.

| attempt | what came back | what was asked for |
|---|---|---|
| `attempt-1-photoreal-pier.jpg` | a photorealistic night pier under a stone arch, a distant boat, harbour lights, a watermark | watercolour; one bollard close up; no boats |
| `attempt-2-pencil-log.jpg` | a pencil-and-wash drawing of a log's end grain with visible growth rings, a pencil lying beside it | a bollard with rope; torchlight; watercolour on heavy paper |

Neither is the picture. Both are *adjacent* to it — a night harbour, a drawing on paper — which is worse than a
blank space, because a reader would take the caption at its word and the plate would contradict the piece it
sits under. That is the failure this bench spends its days on: an artifact that looks like evidence and is not.

So the ask stays unfulfilled, and the magazine prints it as an unfulfilled ask. The lesson is not "the
generator is bad"; it is that **a commission needs a person with taste and a tool they know**, which is exactly
what Slow mode on Mage or the generator inside Google Docs provides. The seam is built and tested; the taste is
not automatable and should not pretend to be.
