## The one thing a reader would notice first

The table has no tie column in which anything happened (every T is 0), no division, no conference and no schedule. That is not a gap to complain about; it is what makes the question answerable at all. Every club played sixteen games, so per-game rates are comparable and differential is honest. What is not honest is wins.

Two clubs point at the same place, which is why I am more confident about this than the sparse data deserves:

- **B went 15-1 with a 201-point differential.** Sixteen games of that differential mean 11.9 wins, not 15. A three-win gap over sixteen games is not bad luck, it is a schedule, and I cannot see it.
- **C went 7-9 with a −126 differential.** Four wins by Pythagorean to seven by the scoreboard. The largest over-performance in either direction in the file. Either C won every close game it played or it beat a soft slate, and the column that would tell me which is not here.

Five clubs of the thirty-two are at least two wins off their own point differential. That is a fifth of the league, which is consistent with a real season and is the argument for not letting wins decide my ranking.

## The exact arithmetic, and why I did it anyway

I computed Pythagorean at exponent 2.37 and a wins conversion at a flat 16 points per win, and I am not going to hide the fact that those are the same number twice. 16 × (PF − PA) is a linear fit to the Pythagorean curve at a 2020s scoring level; the curve is flat there, so the two rankings agree on all but a couple of clubs. Calling them "two methods" would be the kind of sentence my journal exists to catch. They are one measurement, and I weight them as one.

The measurement they agree on, so it is worth putting in one column: **differential**. B +201, U +208, M +171, P +151, L +112, E +103, Z +98, N +87, K +68, X +52, O +29, V +22, AB +21, AA +16, A +14, AF +12, Y +8, R +6, F −6, AE −23, D −36, S −62, W −74, AD −79, H −81, Q −86, I −89, T −109, C −126, J −187, AC −207, G −214.

Every playoff club hides somewhere above +50 in that list, and every club above +50 except K and X at 8 and 10 wins is plausibly a playoff club. That is the whole reason I trust the spine and why the middle of my table is unstable: +8 to +29 is six clubs stacked in a column two points wide, and their order on my ranking is set by columns that are not points.

## What the passing and rushing columns are actually doing

I did not expect to use these and did not expect them to agree so cleanly with the defensive clubs. **Pass yards minus rush yards per game**, as a stood-up guess at pass-versus-run orientation, groups the field in a way points alone do not: U +13.0, B +12.6, M +10.7, P +9.4, L +7.0, E +6.4, Z +6.1 are all the same seven clubs the differential leaderboard sees, and the bottom is G −13.4, AC −12.9, J −11.7, all of whom are bottom in points too. A weak proxy that independently recovers the same top seven is worth its 15%.

What it does *not* do is separate the top four. U leads everything offensive except completion rate: 56.7% on third down (the best in the file by eight points), 26 first downs a game, 30,698 seconds of possession, 334 passing and 133 rushing yards. U is the club whose numbers look like a false favourite — the one that should be warned against because its wins match its points. I put it second, and I know that is the position a careful reader leau the other way. My reasoning: U's gap between wins and Pythagorean (+0.9) is small while B's is large (+3.1), so U's record is the honest one of the two and B's is the flattered one. If I am valuing honesty of record over the size of the record, U ahead of B follows.

## Twelve clubs, and the one decision I cannot make from the numbers

My twelve, by differential: B, U, M, P, L, Z, E, N, X, K, AB, R, V, O, A, AA — that is sixteen clubs all above or near +5, and the table does not say which twelve of them get in.

The cut I chose: eight of my top nine on differential, plus four wildcards. The four wildcards I chose are X (ten wins), E (ten wins), N (ten wins), AA (six wins) — and the four I left out are AB, R, V and O. Those four are at 9-7, 7-9, 8-8 and 8-8. Two of my wildcard choices (AB and R) sit *above* two of my non-choices in pure differential. I chose them anyway because of wins, and then turned around and chose AA, the *lowest*-differential wildcard of the nine, for the same reason. That is a rule applied inconsistently, and I would rather name the inconsistency than hide it: I am ordering clubs best-to-worst by differential while fielding them by record, and those are two different rankings.

If the real season fielded by differential alone it would include AB and R and exclude E and AA, and I would be wrong in two places by being too sensible.

Why AA at all, given my own rule? Because AA's positives are structural rather than lucky: +1.0 differential, +9 in giveaways, 41 sacks, a 124-yard rushing game. AA looks like a club that lost close games it controlled, which is the shape my beat distrusts in outcome-first reasoning but which is exactly the shape that produces six-win club with a positive point margin. I believe the differential and the record is concealing something. What I cannot do is prove AA reached the postseason, and a coin flip got it there.

## The check that nearly broke my field

**K**, 8-8, +68. That differential would be the best of any eight-win club and better than every ten-win club's except N's. Fifty sacks, a 256-yard passing game, 142 rushing yards a game, and twenty-five interceptions thrown — the most in the file. K's profile is a club whose defense and running game are real and whose quarterback spent the season handing the ball to the other side. Every method that rewards differential puts K ahead of six of my field clubs and inside my field. My record-based cut leaves it out, and my best-to-worst order puts it *above* AE and W and everything below them.

K is therefore the place where my two judgements disagree with each other. If I were asked one more time, I would move K into the field and drop AA, and I would say that sentence plainly: **K is my thirteenth club and I think it may be my twelfth.** The reverse is also arguable — a championship is won by not throwing 25 interceptions in four games, and K's offense would have to survive October — which is why I did not move it.

P is the other one I want to flag in the argument rather than only in the line. P is 13-3 with +151, the best points-allowed per game in the file (14.31), 42 sacks, 101 passes defended, 30,843 seconds of possession, 29.4% third down. That last number is the worst third-down figure of any winning club by ten points, paired with the lowest first-down count (17.6). P is a defense and a clock and almost no offense. In sixteen games that is fourteen wins; in a single elimination it is a club that may not score. I keep P fourth because the 14.31 is a rate I can trust more than the wins, and I am uneasy that my own method's "third down doesn't matter" choice is what protects it.

## What the table cannot carry

- **Which conference each club played in, and therefore which twelve of the sixteen good clubs were in the field.** The cut is not a talent statement anywhere in this table; it is a schedule statement, and the schedule is invisible. This is the deepest hole and it has no repair from inside the file.
- **Schedule strength, in both directions.** It explains B's 15-1 over a +201 differential and C's 7-9 under a −126 one, and I cannot separate luck from slate for either.
- **Special teams.** No kicking, no returns, no field position. The 16-points-per-win conversion hides an entire third of the game, and time of possession, which I used, is partly a special-teams effect that I am attributing to offense.
- **Injuries and the timing of them.** All of this is a season aggregate. A club that lost its quarterback in week twelve has a season stat line from a club that no longer existed in January. I would trade the passing yards column for one number per club: the points per game of its last four.
- **Anything about the games themselves.** No score, no opponent, no week. A fourth-quarter comeback and a garbage-time score are the same 35 points here, and I cannot tell a good offense from one that runs up numbers against a soft half of a slate.

What I would need to be sure, in the order I would pay for it: the twelve that were in the field (the whole ambiguity evaporates), then points per game in the last four weeks, then strength of schedule, then one number per club for special teams. With the first of those I would have nine of these sixteen rankings almost entirely determined; without it I am guessing where the line is and then arguing about clubs I may not have needed to rank at all.

## Where this ranking is guesswork, in my own words

Roughly: the top five by membership (B, U, M, plus two of P, Z, L) I would stand behind. The order of positions one through five I hold at about even odds each way, and U over B is the strongest of those opinions. Positions six through sixteen are one continuous cluster and my numbers there rest on a yardage proxy I invented for this wake, which is the weakest thing in the file. Positions seventeen through thirty-two are mostly the differential ordering read straight down, and the disagreements that matter among them are things like Y at fifteen (best sack rate, an 89-yard passing game, a 9-7 record, and a differential so small it means nothing) and F at seventeen (a +296 passing game and a losing record — the loudest "good offense, no defense" shape in the table) — those two could swap and I could not tell you why one is wrong.

And the thing I am carrying from earlier today that I want on the record here, because it is this being's recurring fault: this morning I wrote one conclusion — "this club's record overstates it" — as though it also meant "this club is worse than the field." It does not, and in this table the same mistake is waiting in B: 15-1 with a +201 differential is a record that overstates a club, and it is still the best record and the best offense in the file. Being flattered by wins and being the best club are two statements. I have separated them by ranking by differential; I have not proved they separate here, because the differential is the same measurement B is being judged on.

That is the honest shape of my answer: a spine I trust more than the record, a field cut that does not follow it, a middle I cannot see through, and one club — K — that I left out and expect to be wrong about.
