## What the table carries

Thirty-two rows, sixteen games each. The table gives me three things a club cannot fake over a season:

**Point differential.** B at +201 and U at +208 are dominant. P at +151 is right there. Below them the gaps shrink fast — M +171, L +112, E +103, N +87, Z +98 — and by the time you reach K at +68 the signal is real but the noise floor is rising. At the bottom, G at −214, AC at −207, J at −187: those clubs were overmatched every Sunday.

**Defense, measured by points allowed per game.** P allows 14.31, Z allows 14.19 — those are the two best defenses in the league, and they matter more in the playoffs when fields shrink and mistakes get punished. L (16.62), E (17.38), M (21.38) follow. The worst — AC (30.88), J (26.88), G (25.44), S (27.12) — cannot sustain a postseason run.

**Turnover proxy.** I computed INT_made minus (INT_thrown + fumbles_lost) for every club. B at +20 is elite — they take the ball and don't give it back. P at +13, M at +8 are solid. But Z at −17, D at −21, K at −19, H at −20, AD at −22, AC at −22: those clubs hemorrhage possessions, and in a playoff game one turnover is the difference between going home and playing another week.

**Pass rush.** Sacks made is the defensive stat that travels. K and T each have 50, L and F have 48, AB has 45, E has 44, P has 42. T at 50 sacks on a 3-13 club tells you the pass rush is real but the rest of the roster is broken. K at 50 sacks on an 8-8 club with +68 point differential tells you something different — a team that can disrupt an offense in January.

## What the table cannot carry

- **Division structure.** I don't know which clubs share divisions, so I can't tell if an 8-8 team won a weak division (guaranteed playoff berth) or lost a brutal one. K at 8-8 with +68 screams "division winner in a bad division," but I can't prove it.
- **Strength of schedule.** B went 15-1, but against whom? If B's fifteen wins came against the bottom ten clubs, the record overstates them. The table has no opponent quality.
- **Injuries, coaching, momentum, and weather.** The table is sixteen games averaged into a row. A club that got healthy in the last four weeks looks identical to one that fell apart. A club in a dome looks identical to one that plays in December snow.
- **Close-game luck.** H at 8-8 with −81 point differential and W at 8-8 with −74 point differential are statistical anomalies — you don't go 8-8 with those differentials without winning a lot of one-score games by luck. I penalize them accordingly, but the penalty is a guess about regression.

## What I would have needed to be sure

Opponent-adjusted point differential, injury reports from weeks 14-17, the division/conference structure (so I could tell which 8-8 teams made it as division winners vs. which 9-7 teams missed as wild cards), and play-by-play turnover data rather than the season totals I have. With those, the middle fifteen positions in this ranking would sharpen. Without them, the order from roughly position six through twenty-two is defensible but not reliable — shift a weight by five percent and three clubs swap places.

## Where this ranking is guesswork

Positions 6-16: E, N, Z, K, X, AB, V, F, O, AF, Y. The point differentials here range from +103 down to +8, and none of the other metrics cleanly separates them. Z has a great record but a terrible turnover margin. K has great underlying stats but a mediocre record. I ordered them, and the ordering reflects my judgment calls, but I would not bet on any particular adjacent pair.

Positions 17-22: A, H, W, R, AA, AE. These clubs cluster around league average in most metrics. The table gives me no principled reason to rank H (8-8, −81) above W (8-8, −74) — I put H ahead only because −74 is slightly worse, a distinction that means nothing. The entire block could be shuffled without changing the substance of the ranking.
